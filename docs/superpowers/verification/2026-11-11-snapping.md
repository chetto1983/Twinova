# Verification: the editor, second part — snapping by interfaces (ROADMAP 4.5b, 2026-11-11)

Spec: `2026-11-11-snapping-design.md` (approved, amended for the gap and for the joins' precision). Plan:
`2026-11-11-snapping.md`. Machine: the cloud container with 4 cores. CI: the 4.5b pull request.

## What runs

- **A belt's ends are its drum faces** (decision 7): its deck, guards, exit wall and sink reach 85 mm past its run, as
  OIP's collider does and as the view drew them. The five fixtures were re-spaced by a script so their joins stay face to
  face.
- **Interfaces** (`physics/src/interfaces.rs`): each instance's ends, and a conveyor's rail, built by the layout's own
  code with the instance at the origin. A mounted device's side exit is given in its host's frame.
- **Joins** (`physics/src/snap.rs`, shared by the app and the agent):
  - `join` poses a part so that one of its ends faces another's across a gap of 0 to 0.15 m, matches a conveyor's
    height, and sets the flow;
  - `merge` poses a spur square onto a straight's flank;
  - `moved` drops the joins a move breaks.
- **The format and Build:** a `merge` connection. Build opens the host's guard over the spur's width and refuses a merge
  that does not meet its flank.
- **The view** (`snapping.ts`):
  - candidates while the gizmo drags, with OIP's thresholds; the join is marked and named;
  - release makes the join; Alt places freely;
  - a mounted device has the gizmo; the gap is set beside the view and per flow in the inspector.
- **The agent:** `describe` lists each part's interfaces, a `join` tool, and `move_instance` drops broken joins.

## Found by measuring

- **Real conveyors are not butted** (the user). OIP joins the real faces, and its belt collider reaches the drum face.
  Twinova's physics stopped at the run while its view drew the drums, so butted fixtures drew 85 to 170 mm of overlap.
  The physics now reaches the faces.
- **The M3 demo's jam moved.** The exit wall now stands at the drum face, 3.085 m, so the carton held there clears the
  photocell at 2.75 m. The jam is tripped by the second carton, held behind it, at 8.94 s instead of about 6.8 s.
- **A square merge needs the host's guard opened:** 0 of 3 cartons crossed a closed guard in the probe. The merge
  connection opens it.
- **f32 rounding at a height's range.** A matched height could come out a hair past its parameter's range: 3.0 m read
  as 3.0000000000000004 m. Heights are rounded to the micrometre.
- **A join's turn was stored as `[0, 0, 0, -1]`,** the same rotation as `[0, 0, 0, 1]`. Turns are stored with w ≥ 0.
- **`interfaces_view` was tested only from the app's crate,** and cargo-mutants runs only the mutated crate's tests. A
  physics test now covers it.
- **The E2E's own geometry:**
  - "Frame all" frames what exists, so a drag towards a far end goes in hops, each released where it stays on the
    canvas;
  - the gizmo is grabbed by its arrow behind the move (three draws both);
  - the catalogue column scrolls as the inspector grows, so its button is brought into view first.

## What this does not prove

- **A drum's curve:**
  - the decks are boxes to the face, and the V between two drums is not modelled;
  - a gap is a flat hole, harsher than a real drum.
- **Products squared after a merge:** they turn 25–32° (26.1° on the merge line). Angled merges and powered rollers are a
  later phase.
- **One undo step for a family dropped from the catalogue that snaps:** it takes two, a place and then a join. It has no
  interfaces before Rust places it.
- **A mount distance set by a drop to the millimetre:** a drop lands where the pointer is, about 10 mm a pixel when the
  whole line is framed. The inspector sets it exactly.
- **Moving a joined chain together, reversing a conveyor on a join, grid snapping, families from CAD:** out of 4.5b, as
  the spec says.
- **Windows:** the E2E runs on windows-2022 in CI on the pull request.
